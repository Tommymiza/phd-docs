# Summary of the article "Modelling with stakeholders"

## Context and Objectives

Stakeholder engagement in environmental modeling has become essential, although often superficial in practice. The authors examine different types of participatory modeling and compare these approaches to other frameworks involving stakeholder participation.

## Main Participatory Modeling Approaches

The article identifies several distinct methods:

- **Group Model Building (GMB)**: Dutch approach using causal diagrams and visual tools
- **Mediated Modelling (MM)**: Focused on environmental applications, based on system dynamics
- **Companion Modelling (CM)**: Combining multi-agent models and role-playing games
- **Shared Vision Planning (SVP)**: Developed by the U.S. Army for water management
- **Participatory Simulation (PS)**: Using simulation games for resource optimization

## Primary Objectives

Participatory modeling aims at two objectives: increasing and sharing knowledge about a system and its dynamics, and identifying the impacts of solutions to a given problem to support decision-making.

## Core Principles

The authors propose several key principles:

- **Flexibility**: Prioritizing process over final product
- **Openness**: Maintaining scientific and societal transparency
- **Iteration**: Accepting a cyclical process with backward steps
- **Uncertainty**: Recognizing that uncertainty is perceived differently in social systems
- **New success criteria**: Group validation becomes more important than traditional metrics

## Lessons Learned

### Social Aspects

- Identifying and engaging the right stakeholders from the start is crucial
- The scientist's role as a neutral facilitator is essential
- Managing conflicts and surprises requires particular attention

### Methodological Aspects

- Tool selection should be guided by participants' objectives, not scientists' preferences
- Models must remain transparent and simple enough to be understood
- Collective interpretation of results reinforces ownership of the process

## Conclusions

Participatory modeling helps create common ground for decision-making by providing a shared knowledge pool, improves communication between stakeholder groups, and leads to greater consensus. The authors emphasize the importance of developing adaptive management accompanied by adaptive modeling, recognizing that the collaborative process is often more valuable than the final model itself.
